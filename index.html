Moraine Park

Moraine Park is a large open meadow at 8,160 feet in elevation. The Big Thompson river runs through the valley and it is home to numerous species of birds, mammals and fish.

Rocky Mountain National Park gets a huge influx of visitors during the elk mating season, also known as the rut. Elk are commonly seen in the park's meadows during the evenings, and visitors flock to observe them. So the park calls on the Elk Bugle Corps volunteers.

Three elk are standing in a grassy field, with two of them locking antlers in a display of dominance while the third stands nearby.

Primarily from mid-September through mid-October, prime bull elk — about 8- to 9-years-old — battle each other for the right to establish harems of cows with which to breed.

About the bugling: It's a signal of a bull elk's strength. The bugling generally starts with a crescendo of deep, resonant tones that rise rapidly to a high-pitched squeal before dropping to a series of grunts. The bigger the bull, the louder and more range the call.

Cub Lake Trailhead

As you cross the Big Thompson river, keep an eye out for trout, such as wild brown, brook, and greenback cutthroat.

Changing weather

Be prepared for a variety of weather conditions, no matter the time of year. if you are prepared, you can enjoy the unexpected pleasure of a cool fog, a snowstorm, or a rain shower.

A chorus of frogs

As you pass through wetlands, such as here at Cub Lake, listen for the calls of male Western Chorus Frogs.

Western terrestrial garter snake

In the park's lower elevations, you may come across a western terrestrial garter snake warming up in the sun.

Dusky grouse

In the late spring and summer, dusky grouse - such as this chick- can be spotted in shrubby areas.

Wildfire recovery

In 2020, the East Troublesome Fire reached this area, inflicting major damage. Although you can still see evidence of the fire, such as with these blackened tree trunks, you can also see the regrowth of vegetation.

Ouzel Falls

Hike along a gentle path by the side of North Saint Vrain Creek as it makes its way through the deep forests of Wild Basin. The trail passes by the impressive Calypso Cascades and then winds through open forest before climbing up to beautiful Ouzel Falls.

Ouzel Falls powerfully drops onto boulders where it turns and cascades into the valley below. After 0.3mi/0.5mi along the level, forested trail, take the side trail to Copeland Falls where North Saint Vrain Creek drops into a couple of mesmerizing pools.

North Saint Vrain Creek along the Ouzel Falls trail.

Continue up along the playful creek and cross over at an unnamed cascade. A brief climb takes you to another bridge over the Calypso Cascade, your second waterfall of the day. The trail traverses the hillside before climbing up to Ouzel Falls.

Before crossing the bridge, follow the trail up to the base of the falls where it shoots out from the ledge above and drops 40ft/12m.

Trailhead: From Colorado Hwy 7, just north of Allenspark, turn onto Wild Basin Road. The Wild Basin Ranger Station and trailhead is 2.2mi/3.5km past the entrance station.

Sprague Lake

Sprague Lake with Hallett Peak

📏 Distance: 0.7 miles round trip (1.1 Km) ⬆ Elevation Gain: 34 feet 🔄 Type of Trail: Loop, Easy

Stroll around the lake shore, rest on the numerous benches, and take in the fantastic mountain vistas of Rocky Mountain National Park, while enjoying the smooth, wide path on this short hike.

This gorgeous and wheelchair accessible stroll is great for hikers of all abilities. There are plenty of benches and lookouts over the lake to stop and reflect. Enjoy the view - mountain peaks along the Continental Divide can be seen clearly from the east shoreline.
